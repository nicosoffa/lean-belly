The medical literature on female metabolism after menopause tells a very specific story — one that most GPs don't have time to explain. Two biological processes compound each other to create what researchers call "metabolic resistance" — a state where the body actively resists fat loss regardless of how little you eat.

Research Reference

A 2023 study from Keio University School of Medicine found that postmenopausal women experience accelerated mitochondrial efficiency decline — the cellular process that determines whether calories become energy or stored fat. Average decline: 2–3% per year after 45. At 55, a woman may be storing 20–30% more fat per calorie than at 40 — with zero change in diet or exercise.

But mitochondrial decline is only half the story. The second factor is elevated ceramides — lipid molecules that accumulate in the bloodstream and coat fat cells with a barrier that resists breakdown. Think of ceramides as a padlock around stored fat. Even with a caloric deficit, high ceramide levels mean fat cells simply don't respond normally.

The two-mechanism cycle that standard diets were never designed to address.

Plain language: You are not failing the diet. The diet was designed for a 35-year-old body. After the hormonal shift of perimenopause, your cells operate under different rules. No standard diet addresses those rules — and no amount of willpower changes biology.
